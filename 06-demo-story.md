# Demo Story: Pitches & Walkthrough Guide (Judge-Aware)

This guide gives you three versions of your pitch—30 seconds, 60 seconds, and a full 5–7 minute walkthrough—shaped for this specific judge panel.

Practice until it feels natural. Don't memorize word-for-word; use these as guides.

**Key principle:** Frame Afterglow as a *browsing and organization tool* that respects DJ artistry, not as a recommendation engine.

---

## The 30-Second Pitch (Attention & Hook)

**Use this when:**
- A judge asks "What are you building?"
- You have limited time before diving into the demo

**Your goal:** Describe the problem vividly. Hint at a different approach that respects DJ thinking.

---

### 30-Second Script

> "I'm a DJ, and I play multi-day festivals where I don't know which vibe I'll DJ until I arrive. I could play Afterglow, sunrise, late-night, or daytime. I need to arrive prepared with multiple playlists on a USB—but building them takes hours.
>
> **Afterglow flips that.** Instead of spending hours on separate playlists, I see my entire library organized into vibes. Within each vibe, I navigate by *how I actually think*—role and crowd state, not genre and BPM. Same tracks, completely different prep workflow.
>
> The result: I prep 3–4 vibes in 15 minutes instead of hours. I arrive confident. I can say yes to any moment."

**Timing:** ~30 seconds at natural conversational pace.

**Tone:** Grounded, specific, personal. You're speaking from experience.

**Judge resonance:**
- **Richie Hawtin:** "You're navigating by context"—respects DJ thinking
- **Romain Pouillon:** Specific problem (spontaneous gigs) + practical solution
- **Carlos Caires:** "Context and moment"—musical sophistication
- **JB Thiebaut:** Clear, specific problem statement

## The 60-Second Pitch (Full Context + Invitation)

**Use this when:**
- You have a minute to pitch before the walkthrough
- A judge wants more detail before committing to watch the demo

**Your goal:** Tell the full story. Problem, why it matters, your insight, your approach.

---

### 60-Second Script

> "Most DJ tools force you to build separate playlists for each scenario—Afterglow, sunrise, late-night, daytime—which takes hours. And within each playlist, you navigate by genre and BPM, not by how you actually think.
>
> Here's the real problem: At a festival, you don't know which moment you'll DJ until you arrive. You need to be ready for *anything*—but preparation takes too long.
>
> When you browse by *how you actually navigate*—role and crowd state instead of genre/BPM—within each vibe, everything becomes clear instantly. You're not scrolling lists; you're exploring a map of your own music organized by context.
>
> **Afterglow** organizes your library into vibes, and within each vibe, by performance contexts. Same tracks, radically different prep workflow. You arrive at a festival with Afterglow, Sunrise Reset, Club, and Pool vibes organized and ready. You can say yes to unexpected opportunities. You feel genuinely prepared.
>
> That's what happens when you organize by DJ decision-making, not metadata alone."

**Timing:** ~60 seconds at natural pace.

**Tone:** Narrative, grounded, building to a revelation.

**Structure:**
- Problem statement (metadata mismatch)
- Why it matters (real DJ scenarios)
- Your insight (how DJs actually think)
- Your solution (Afterglow's approach)
- The benefit (speed, rediscovery, confidence)

**Judge resonance:**
- **Richie Hawtin:** Respects DJ thinking over automation
- **Carlos Caires:** "Moment and context"—musical intelligence
- **Romain Pouillon:** Real problem, practical solution

## The 5–7 Minute Walkthrough (Full Demo)

**Use this for:**
- The main hackathon pitch (judges have 5–10 minutes per project)
- Close-up demos at your booth

**Your goal:** Show judges how to prep multiple vibes for uncertain festival scenarios in minutes. Emphasize spatial navigation across contexts, multi-vibe readiness, and DJ agency throughout.

**Timing breakdown:**
- Opening hook (30 sec) — Festival scenario, multi-vibe uncertainty
- Timeline + Constellation intro (30 sec) — Show 7 contexts, click Afterglow
- Spatial logic walkthrough (2.5–3 min) — Explain three zones, spatial relationships
- Clarity moment (1–1.5 min) — Click a track, show why it belongs, tags
- Multi-vibe readiness (45 sec) — Flip to Sunrise Reset, show preparedness
- Close (30 sec) — Festival-ready summary

---

#### Opening (30 seconds)

**[Stand ready, laptop / screen visible. Friendly, conversational. Make eye contact.]**

> "Okay, so picture this. You're heading to a multi-day festival next weekend. You could get booked for Afterglow—that late-night, reflective moment. Or sunrise wind-down. Or you might get pulled into the late-night warehouse stage. You don't know until you arrive.
>
> You need to prep 3–4 different vibes on a USB—different energy, different pace, different moment. Most people open Spotify and start building playlists. It takes hours. You're stressed.
>
> **Afterglow** is designed for exactly this moment. Let me show you how to see all your prepared vibes at once, and navigate each one."

**[Brief, engaging, sets up the real uncertainty DJs face.]**

**Judge resonance:**
- **Richie:** Personal, authentic—respects real DJ scenarios
- **Romain:** Multi-scenario prep challenge
- **Michele:** Human-centered problem statement

---

#### Setup the Scenario (30 seconds)

**[Point to the screen. Open Afterglow. Timeline view should be visible.]**

> "You open Afterglow. The first thing you see is a timeline—all the different moments you might need to DJ.
>
> Sunrise Reset, Warm-up, Festival Brunch, Pool Party, Afterglow, Club, Late Night Warehouse.
>
> You're most likely to get Afterglow or Sunrise, so you say: 'Let me see what I have for Afterglow first.' You click on it."

**[Click on Afterglow. Constellation view loads. Pause for 2 seconds so judges can take in what they're seeing.]**

**What judges should see:**
- Three visible zones with labels (Arrivals, Lock-in, Wanderers)

- Clear spatial relationships showing different crowd states and journey roles

#### Main Walkthrough: The Constellation (2.5–3 minutes)

**[Stand back slightly. Give judges time to look at the spatial arrangement.]**

> "Now you see something different. Not a list. A map—a spatial arrangement of your **own library for this specific moment** (Afterglow), organized around how you actually navigate when you DJ.
>
> You can see **three zones here**:
>
> On the left, **Arrivals energy**—people are still settling in as the night winds down. You want tracks that invite them in gently, no pressure yet.
>
> In the middle, **Lock-in energy**—people are fully present, dancing, connected to the moment. You can deepen the feeling here; they're with you.
>
> And on the right, **Wanderers**—people are having conversations, moving between spaces, present but not glued to the dance floor. You need music that *frames their experience* without demanding their full attention.
>
> This isn't someone's algorithm. This is **your 70 Afterglow tracks**, organized by the decision-making you make when you perform."

**[Let them look for 5–10 seconds. Gesture through the zones to show spatial relationships.]**

> "Notice: tracks that work in multiple moments—they're positioned between the zones. A track that bridges Arrivals and Lock-in sits right between them. Spatial proximity shows the relationship instantly."

**[Optional: Hover over a track cluster to show nearby tracks fade in/highlight.]**

> "When you hover near a cluster, related tracks become more visible. It's like moving through your own knowledge. The interface responds to where you're exploring."

**[Pause for 3 seconds. Let them absorb the spatial logic.]**

## Common Judge Questions (And How to Answer)

**After your walkthrough, judges might ask these. Have answers ready.**

### Q: "How is this different from Spotify playlists or a good folder structure?"

**Your answer:**
> "Spotify playlists are external and generic. A folder structure is linear—you have to open each folder and read names.
>
> Afterglow organizes *your library* around *your workflow*. You can see everything at once—your whole Pool Party section—and spatial proximity shows you relationships at a glance.
>
> A good folder structure is a good start. Afterglow is what you do *beyond* that: organize around how you actually navigate during performance prep."

---

### Q: "Does this integrate with djay?"

**Your answer:**
> "We designed it to sit *alongside* djay—you organize in Afterglow, then you play in djay (or your preferred player).
>
> Real SDK integration is a post-hackathon goal. For now, the concept is to prove that this *way of thinking about* library organization actually solves the real problem."

---

### Q: "What if someone has 5,000 tracks?"

**Your answer:**
> "The three-layer model scales. With smart filtering and analytic helpers, you can navigate thousands of tracks.
>
> But for the MVP, we're proving the core concept works with a focused demo library. The question isn't 'does it scale?' but 'does this way of organizing actually help DJs?' If yes, then scaling becomes an engineering problem, not a concept problem."

---

### Q: "Who would actually use this?"

**Your answer:**
> "Emerging and casual DJs—people who get booked spontaneously and feel overwhelmed organizing their library.
>
> But also experienced DJs who want to see their library organized by performance context instead of metadata, or explore cross-genre combinations in a more intuitive way.
>
> Anyone who wants to navigate their library by how they actually think about music—by role and crowd state, not just BPM and genre."

---

### Q: "How do tracks get tagged?"

**Your answer:**
> "Right now, manual tagging. You listen to a track and ask three questions:
>
> 'When would I play this?' (Time/Setting)
> 'What does it do in a set?' (Journey Role)
> 'What crowd can it handle?' (Crowd State)
>
> The tagging system reflects how DJs already think. It's not reductive; it's precise.
>
> Post-hackathon, we could suggest tags based on metadata (BPM, genre). But the strength is in honest, intentional tagging."

---

### Q: "Doesn't this risk turning DJing into paint-by-numbers?" (Richie might ask)

**Your answer:**
> "No. This is *organization*, not curation. Your taste and choices remain central.
>
> The system helps you prepare faster and explore your library more deeply. That actually gives you more confidence to experiment and take risks.
>
> If anything, better organization of your own knowledge means you can be more creative and intuitive when you're actually playing."

---

### Q: "Why not use machine learning to suggest tags?" (Carlos, JB might ask)

**Your answer:**
> "We deliberately didn't go the AI route. This is about *you* organizing *your* library, not a system making decisions for you.
>
> Manual tagging proves that DJs think in this way already. If we added ML, we'd obscure that core insight.
>
> Post-hackathon, optional tag suggestions based on metadata could be helpful for *learning*, not decision-making."

---

### Q: "Wouldn't a simple grid of filters do this?" (JB might ask)

**Your answer:**
> "A grid of filters requires you to know what you're looking for and then click through options.
>
> Spatial browsing lets you see *everything at once* and notice things. It supports exploratory prep, not just targeted search.
>
> You're not asking 'do I have a Bridge track?' You're asking 'what do I have *in this moment*?' and discovering material you'd forgotten."

## If Something Goes Wrong During the Demo

**The prototype crashes or something doesn't load:**

> "This is a prototype, so sometimes things break. Let me show you the core interaction on the next view."

**[Switch to the next view or go back and try again. Stay calm and conversational.]**

> "The core idea is the three-layer navigation: timeline → spatial mood map → track details. This glitch doesn't change that insight. The question judges are really evaluating is: does organizing around *performance contexts* actually help DJs? I believe it does."

**Have a backup plan:**
- Screenshots on your phone
- A printed 1-page visual guide
- A second laptop with the demo ready

## Delivery Tips

### Pacing
- Slow down when introducing concepts (judges need time to absorb)
- Speed up during the walkthrough (judges want to see the flow)
- Pause after key moments (rediscovery, the arc) and let them sit
- Don't rush to fill silence; let your words land

### Eye Contact & Body Language
- Make eye contact; don't just look at the screen
- Stand to the side so judges see the demo, not you
- Use natural gestures (point to zones, gesture at spatial relationships)
- Step back when showing the full view; step closer for details
- Let your hands rest between gestures (don't fidget)

### Engagement
- Invite reactions ("Notice how spatial proximity shows relationship?")
- Pause for 3–5 seconds after big moments
- Read the room. If judges look confused, slow down or clarify
- If judges look engaged, keep momentum
- Be authentic; let your care for the problem come through

### Tone
- Conversational, not scripted
- Passionate but grounded (you're a DJ solving a real problem)
- Genuine enthusiasm for the rediscovery moment
- Humor is fine if it's natural, not forced
- Respectful of judges' expertise; invite their perspective
