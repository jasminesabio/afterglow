# The DJ Problem: Why Afterglow Exists

---

## The Core Problem

DJs organize their libraries with technical metadata (genre, BPM, key) because that's what existing tools support.

But when actually preparing to perform, DJs navigate by **emotional and situational logic**:
- **When would I play this?** (time of day, context)
- **Why does this track belong here?** (what role does it play?)
- **What crowd state does this need?** (are people arriving? locked in? wandering?)

This mismatch is especially painful for **emerging and casual DJs in spontaneous scenarios**.

---

## The Friction Points

### Scenario 1: The Multi-Day Event Uncertainty (Burning Man, Festivals)
You're at Burning Man with scheduled gigs, but it's unpredictable. You might get asked to:
- Play a sunrise wind-down at 6 AM (wasn't planned)
- DJ a 4 AM dance floor (spontaneous request)
- Cover someone's brunch slot last-minute
- Play a camp you weren't expecting

You need to feel *ready for anything*, but you don't know exactly what context you'll need.

**Current reality:**
- "I need sunrise material. Let me search my hard drive..."
- Browse folders: "House," "Deep House," "Funk," "World"
- Scroll through 200+ house tracks looking for ones that feel "sunrise"
- Do I have enough ambient stuff? Groovy stuff? Opener energy?
- Spend 30+ minutes in a panic
- Wonder: "Do I actually have good material for this context?"

**What you're actually thinking:**
- "It's 6 AM. People are tired but listening. I need something uplifting but not jarring. What openers do I have for this vibe?"
- "If I get asked for a 4 AM set, what do I have that's deep but danceable?"
- "For different times of day, what's my strongest material? Where are the gaps?"

You're navigating by **context and moment**, not by folder structure. But your tools force you to use metadata.

### Scenario 2: The Forgotten Track
You have thousands of tracks. Great ones get lost because:
- They don't fit your folder structure perfectly
- You haven't played them in 6 months
- They're "too experimental" for your usual stuff, so you file them somewhere and forget

You never stumble on them again.

### Scenario 3: The Confidence Gap
You're asked to play an unfamiliar context: "We need someone for festival brunch" or "It's a Burning Man camp, vibe is uncertain."

Your library suddenly feels overwhelming. You don't know what you have that *fits*. Organization-as-list doesn't help you think through the scenario.

---

## Why This Matters

**An emerging DJ who feels blocked by their organization system stops DJing.**

The mental load of organizing + the stress of spontaneous gigs = reduced confidence and less practice time.

**A confident DJ discovers cross-genre combinations and gets better.** They feel agency over their craft. They take more gigs. They improve faster.

Organization directly impacts:
- Confidence in uncertain situations
- Discovery of forgotten material
- Creativity (seeing connections across genres)
- Willingness to take spontaneous bookings

---

## The Users

### Primary: Emerging & Casual DJs
- Playing 1–4 gigs per month
- 500–5,000 tracks in library
- Often self-booked or spontaneously asked to play
- May feel overwhelmed by existing organization systems
- Thinks in moods and moments, not BPM/genre
- Needs to prep gigs fast (2–3 hour window)

**Why they care:** Reduces mental load. Gives confidence in uncertain scenarios. Makes prep faster.

### Secondary: Experienced DJs
- Want to rediscover underplayed material
- Exploring cross-genre combinations
- Interested in organizing around performance contexts (not just technical metadata)

**Why they care:** New way to think about their library. Discovers material they'd forgotten.

### All Users Share This:
Preparing for **uncertain performance scenarios** (unknown timing, unknown crowd, unknown setup).

---

## The Current Solution Landscape

### What Exists
- **DJ software** (Serato, Rekordbox, djay): Organize by folder, tags, BPM, key
- **Spotify playlists:** Genre-based or mood-based ("Chill House," "Workout")
- **Manual spreadsheets:** Some DJs build their own systems

### What's Missing
No tool organizes a DJ's library by **how they actually prepare for gigs**.

All existing tools optimize for playback, mixing, or generic mood categorization. None optimize for prep + context awareness + spontaneous scenario planning.

---

## The Insight

DJs don't think: "I need a track that's 120 BPM in the key of A minor."

They think: "I need something for 4 pm when people are arriving. It's got to be uplifting and accessible, but not cheesy. It sets the tone. It's an opener."

**If we organize by DJ decision-making dimensions (When? Why? Who's here?) instead of metadata, library browsing becomes discovery instead of searching.**

## Why Now?

- DJ communities are growing (emerging DJs everywhere)
- Spontaneous booking culture is real (residencies are rare; gig economy is normal)
- Existing tools are generic (Spotify, djay, Serato are powerful but not context-aware)
- The problem is specific and solvable (spatial UI + manual curation = better than algorithmic recommendations)

**This isn't about replacing DJ software. It's about solving the prep problem.**

---

## What We're NOT Solving

- Mixing or beatmatching
- Audio playback
- Integration with existing DJ hardware
- Algorithmic recommendations (DJ decides; system organizes)

**What we ARE solving:** The gap between "how DJs think" and "how libraries are organized."
